Why here

Lost Creek is the cheapest way out of Austin into real wilderness. Denver fares run $247 nonstop on Southwest, the cheapest cars on that lot quote under a hundred dollars for three days, and the trailhead is two hours from the terminal. Solo, where nothing splits, those two lines are the difference between a five-hundred-dollar trip and an eight-hundred-dollar one. The schedule cooperates too: a 6:55 AM flight out and a 7:55 PM flight home on day three, so neither end needs a motel.

It also happens to be the right shape for August. This is a wilderness of Pikes Peak granite domes and hoodoos with the trail threading below them at eight to nine thousand feet — under treeline nearly the whole way, in a month when the high country gets hit by lightning most afternoons. Lost Creek itself vanishes into and out of the rock repeatedly, which is the thing people come to see. Water is reliable, camps are established, and the trail is popular enough to be a solo-friendly place to be alone.

  1. Mile 0 · 7,709 ft Goose Creek trailhead End of eleven gravel miles on FR-211, next to Goose Creek Campground. Parking and horse facilities, and a permit board. The self-issue wilderness permit here is mandatory, not courtesy — Lost Creek is one of the few Colorado wildernesses that requires one. It is free. Elevation is the USGS 10-metre DEM value at the Forest Service coordinate; the agency lists the adjacent campground at 7,723 ft, which agrees.
  2. The first decision Goose Creek #612, not Hankins Pass #630 Two trails leave this trailhead. Take Goose Creek Trail #612, north up the creek. Hankins Pass Trail #630 climbs southwest toward a 10,010-foot pass — a fine trail and entirely the wrong direction for this trip.
  3. Miles 0–4.5 Up the valley, looking for a legal site The trail winds north with the creek through Pikes Peak granite. Water throughout. The Forest Service's own warning is worth repeating: the lower valley is tight and good legal campsites are scarce, because the wilderness order requires 100 feet of separation from lakes, streams, and trails alike. Walk until you find one rather than settling for an illegal spot near the water.
  4. Mile 3.8 · the side trail The Shafthouse A signed spur leads to the standing bunkhouses and the remains of the shaft house — the 1890s works of the Antero and Lost Park Reservoir Company, who tried to dam Lost Creek and discovered that a creek which flows through granite rather than over it cannot be impounded. The most-walked destination in this half of the wilderness, and a reasonable turnaround in itself: about 10.6 miles round trip from the trailhead if you add the creek below the buildings.
  5. Above the Shafthouse Refrigerator Gulch and the sinks Where Lost Creek does the thing it is named for — vanishing into the rock and surfacing again downstream, with domes and hoodoos above. This is the day-two wander, done with a light pack from camp. Distances beyond the Shafthouse are not well sourced; treat the map and the weather as the limit, not a mileage target.
  6. Mile 9.7 · 9,548 ft Wigwam Park, if you go the distance Goose Creek #612 ends here at the junction with Wigwam Trail #609 — 9.7 miles from the trailhead, with 2,377 feet of cumulative gain and a 10,120-foot high point along the way. Reaching it and returning makes for a long second day; it is the stretch goal, not the plan.
  7. Back to 7,709 ft The return Straight back down the valley to the trailhead — losing what you climbed, on a trail you have already read once. Sign out if there's a register. The gravel road is the last obstacle, and it is easier in daylight going out than it was coming in.

A tight canyon, a hard rule, and nobody to send

This is the gentlest of the three options and still not casual alone. Its specific problems are a camping rule that is easy to break, a creek canyon that concentrates weather and water, and the ordinary solo arithmetic of having no second person.

  • 100 feet from water and trail, both. Lost Creek's wilderness order (in force through 2027) sets the setback at 100 feet from lakes, streams and trails for camping, fires, and tethering. Sites meeting it are scarce in the lower canyon — plan to walk past the obvious spots.
  • Satellite messenger on your body, check-in schedule someone is watching, and the route, camp, plate and walk-out deadline left at home. No signal in the canyon.
  • Stoves only, and check the order. Stage 2 restrictions covered this district when checked and allow only liquid or gas devices in a three-foot cleared area. The order as written expired August 1 — assume restrictions and verify.
  • Storms funnel here. Being below treeline helps with lightning but not with flash runoff in a tight granite canyon. Don't camp in the streambed, however flat and inviting.
  • The road is the weak link. Eleven gravel miles in an ordinary rental, no signal, no partner vehicle. Drive it in daylight both ways and don't try it after heavy rain.

Bring the permit discipline and the campsite discipline and this is about as forgiving as western backpacking gets alone.

Before we go

Fires

Stage 2 restrictions were in effect across the South Park and South Platte districts from June 30, 2026, allowing only liquid- or gas-fuelled devices in a cleared three-foot area — no campfires or charcoal. The order as signed ran through August 1, so its status for these dates is genuinely unknown; it may well be re-issued. Plan stove-only and call.

Permit

Lost Creek requires a mandatory free self-issue wilderness permit, which is unusual for Colorado. Fill it in at the trailhead board — this is the detail most people miss.

Water

Goose Creek runs alongside the trail for most of the route. Reliable, and worth filtering.

Altitude

The mildest of the three trips: trailhead 7,709 feet and camps around 8,600. Coming from Austin you will still notice it, but this is the option that doesn't punish a same-day arrival.

The road in

Eleven miles of unpaved FR-211 (Matukat Road) after 42 miles of pavement. The Forest Service posts no high-clearance requirement, which is why this trip works with the cheapest car on the lot — but it's washboard, and wet weather changes the calculation.

Other rules

Group limit 15 people (25 combined with pack animals), dogs on a leash, no bicycles or game carts, pack out all refuse, nothing left unattended more than 72 hours. All from the wilderness order in force through December 31, 2027.
