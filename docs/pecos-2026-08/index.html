Why here

The Pecos is the closest real alpine wilderness to Austin — one 50-minute nonstop to Albuquerque, two hours of driving, and you're walking at 9,500 feet under 12,000-foot granite with no permit, no quota, no lottery, and no grizzlies. Late August is the tail of the monsoon: the high country is as green as it gets, the aspens are a couple of weeks from turning, and the crowds thin once school starts.

The route is the back door, and this year that's not a style choice. The front-door trailhead at Jacks Creek — the busy way in — is closed through September 30, 2026 for construction, along with Panchuela and Field Tract. Iron Gate, at the end of five rough miles of forest road, stays open and puts us on Hamilton Mesa: a long open meadow crest with the whole Truchas skyline stood up in front of you. It's a longer walk to the same lake, and a much quieter one.

The route, junction by junction

Three turns decide this route, and two of them are in the first four miles. Get the mesa forks right, take the signed left onto Larkspur, and find the one fork below Round Mountain where right means the lake and left means a closed trailhead.

  1. Mile 0 · ~9,480 ft Iron Gate trailhead End of five rough miles of FR 223 — the Forest Service says high clearance, and means it. $5 a day per vehicle at the board, no wilderness permit needed. No water here, so day one's supply rides up from town. Hamilton Mesa Trail #249 leaves from the top of the campground loop.
  2. Miles 0.5–1.0 · two forks Bear left, twice Two forks come quickly on the climb to the mesa — stay left both times on #249. Right at the second fork is Rociada Trail #250, which drops three miles off the east side to Mora Flats — wrong drainage, and every foot has to be re-climbed. Sources disagree on whether the first fork sits at a half mile or a full mile; expect it early and read the signs.
  3. Miles 1–4 · the mesa Hamilton Mesa A long open meadow crest around 10,000 feet with the Truchas group stood up across the valley — the best sustained view of the trip, and the reason to be glad the short way in was closed. It is also dry: the only water reported up here is stock tanks. Walk it early if storms are building.
  4. Mile 4.2 · signed junction Left onto Larkspur #260 The signed junction is the trip's key turn: left and downhill, west, onto Larkspur Trail #260. Continuing north on #249 heads for Pecos Falls, miles the wrong way. On the descent, Bob Grounds Trail #270 comes in — keep straight downhill.
  5. Mile 5.8 · ~9,600 ft Beatty's Cabin — Camp 1 Bridge over the Pecos River under limestone cliffs. Camping and fires are prohibited at Beatty's Flats itself — take a site along the trail outside the flats, 50 feet off trail and water. First reliable water since the car; tank up for the morning.
  6. Miles 5.8–9.3 · the dogleg Southwest on Beatty's #25 Cross the bridge and follow Beatty's Trail #25 southwest over the shoulder of Round Mountain — away from the lake on the map, which is correct and the price of the open trailhead. This segment's length (~3.5 mi) is derived from descriptions, not measured; trust the junctions over the mileage. At the fork below Round Mountain, right and north onto Jacks Creek Trail #257. Left goes 2.5 miles down to the closed Jacks Creek trailhead.
  7. Miles 9.3–14 · the climb Up Jacks Creek to the lake The real work: about 1,500 feet over the last few miles, timber giving way as East Pecos Baldy fills the sky. Dockweiler #259 branches east partway up — stay on #257. Just below the lake the trail splits: left and northwest to the water; right is #257 climbing Trailriders Ridge.
  8. Mile ~14 · 11,420 ft Pecos Baldy Lake — Camp 2 The lake sits in a bowl under East Pecos Baldy at 11,420 ft. No camping in the basin itself — established sites are in the trees north and southwest, 200 feet back from the water. The resident bighorn sheep are habituated and will work an unattended camp; keep the tent zipped and food hung.
  9. The extension we're not doing Truchas Lakes, 5.2 miles on Skyline Trail #251 runs north from the lake over Trailriders Wall — a long exposed ridge marked by chest-high cairns — to the Truchas Lakes at 11,867 ft. On a two-night schedule from Iron Gate it doesn't fit; with a third night it's the obvious move. Water between the lakes is scarce, and the Wall is nowhere to be at 2 PM in August.
  10. Miles 14–28 · back over the top The return, and its one trap Reverse the whole line. The trap is right at the start: from the lake take #257 south, downhill — not up Trailriders Ridge, not Skyline. At the Round Mountain fork bear left for Beatty's, refill at the bridge, then climb Larkspur's ~700 feet back to the mesa with tired legs before the last easy four miles to the car. Budget the whole day.

Lightning, and a dry mesa

No grizzlies here — the thing that kills people in the Pecos in August is weather. It's monsoon season: storms build over the divide most afternoons, and half this route is open mesa or exposed climb with nowhere to be small.

  • Off open ground by early afternoon. Hamilton Mesa, the upper #257 climb, East Pecos Baldy, and Trailriders Wall are all exposed. Summits happen by lunch or not at all.
  • Carry the mesa dry. No water at the trailhead or for the first 5.8 miles — start with 2–3 liters each from town. Refill at the Pecos River bridge and the lake. Filter everything.
  • Hang everything scented. Black-bear country with a drought pushing bears toward camps. No canister rule — a proper hang or Ursack, 200 ft from the tent.
  • The bighorns are bold. Habituated sheep patrol the lake camps — zip the tent, don't leave packs open, and never feed them.
  • The road is part of the route. Five miles of rough FR 223, one livestock gate, no signal from Pecos village onward. Drive it in daylight.

None of this is exotic. It's a noon turnaround discipline and a water plan — they just both have to actually happen.

Before we go

Fires

Stage II restrictions (no wood fires anywhere, stoves only in a cleared area) were ordered forest-wide June 26 through September 30, 2026 — though the forest's alert page had stopped listing the order when checked July 28, with no rescission announced. Plan stove-only regardless and check the Santa Fe NF alerts page the week we leave.

Water

The Pecos River at Beatty's and the lake are reliable; everything between is not, in a drought year. The mesa is dry and the side streams are suspect — carry accordingly and filter.

Altitude

Austin is at 500 feet; camp two is at 11,420. Camp one at ~9,600 is deliberately the acclimatization night. Drink more than feels necessary and expect the #257 climb to feel unfair.

The road in

NM-63 is paved to the Iron Gate turnoff; FR 223's five miles are rough dirt the Forest Service flags for high-clearance vehicles. There may be a closed livestock gate — pass through and re-close it. Tight, slow, fine in the SUV when dry.

No signal

Nothing past Pecos village. Someone at home gets the full itinerary, and a satellite messenger comes if any of us has one.

Why not Jacks Creek

The normal trailhead for this lake is closed May 4 – September 30, 2026 for construction (Panchuela and Field Tract too), and the closure order covers being on the road at all. If it's rescinded early the trip gets 7 miles shorter each way — call the district before assuming.
