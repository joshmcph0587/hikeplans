Five lakes,
no exposure

Seventeen miles through the Gore Range on a loop whose high point is a lake, not a pass — five alpine lakes, water the whole way, and every camp in the trees. Josh, Jon and Pat — August 24 to 27, 2026.

Every elevation here is a USGS 3DEP reading at a surveyed coordinate — the lakes and trailheads from OpenStreetMap, the junctions computed where the trail geometries actually meet. Why here

This is the trip the other candidates kept failing at. Alpine lakes and big views normally mean going above treeline, which means a dawn start, a dry traverse and nowhere to shelter when the sky turns over — that is what ruled out the Lost Creek loop and the Rollins Pass loop before it. The Gore Range breaks the pattern, because its lakes sit in timbered basins at 10,000 to 10,750 feet with thirteen-thousand-foot peaks standing directly over them. You get the drama by looking up instead of by standing on top of it. Five lakes you can legally camp at, water within reach nearly the whole way, and 2,800 feet of climbing across three days.

Late August is the right window. The creek crossings on the Surprise Trail run high with snowmelt in June and are low by now, and 2026's record drought — the thing that makes the Lost Creek plan a gamble — matters far less on a route whose water is a chain of lakes rather than one headwater stream. The cost is that this is the highest first night of any plan we have looked at: 10,752 feet, straight from Austin. That is the one thing to plan around.

The route, junction by junction

Four trails and three junctions, none of them ambiguous once you know the numbers: Surprise Trail #62 up, Gore Range Trail #60 across, the Upper Cataract–Mirror Lake Trail #63 out to Camp 1 and back, then Eaglesmere Trail #61 down. The one thing to get right is that the spur to Upper Cataract Lake is a there-and-back — you return to the same junction.

  1. Mile 0 · 8,585 ft Surprise trailhead End of 2.4 miles of improved dirt past Cataract Creek Campground. Two things happen here: $5 into the pay station, and your name in the wilderness register. The fee covers trails and recreation use for five consecutive days; the permit is free but mandatory. No potable water and no toilet at the lot — the nearest vault is a mile back at Lower Cataract Lake. The trail crosses Cataract Creek on a bridge and starts climbing.
  2. Miles 0–3.3 Up the Surprise Trail #62 Fourteen hundred feet of switchbacked climb through timber, with several creek crossings early. They run high with snowmelt in June and should be straightforward in late August. The trail was rerouted around mile 2.8 and a 2024 report through here logged significant deadfall, so expect obstacles. This is the stretch that does most of the day's work.
  3. Mile 3.3 · 9,981 ft Left onto the Gore Range Trail #60 The Surprise Trail ends at the Gore Range Trail. Turn left. Surprise Lake opens up 0.2 miles along at 10,057 feet, and this is where the day hikers stop — the trip reports that describe seeing three people all day are describing what happens past this point.
  4. Mile 4.1 · 10,391 ft Right onto the Upper Cataract–Mirror Lake Trail #63 Eight tenths of a mile west of the Surprise junction, trail #63 leaves south. Turn right. Fix this junction in your head: you walk back to it tomorrow at mile 8.1 and carry on west, and it is the only place on the loop where you retrace ground. Staying on #60 here skips Camp 1 entirely and turns the trip into the standard twelve-mile loop.
  5. Mile 6.1 · 10,752 ft Upper Cataract Lake — Camp 1 Two miles of climbing on #63, measured along the mapped trail, to the highest point of the loop — which is a lake with Mount Powell, 13,586 feet, standing over it. Camp 100 feet from water and trail. No campfire here under any circumstances: the wilderness order prohibits them within a quarter mile of any lake, and this is a lake basin. Stove only, and that is before the forest-wide fire restrictions.
  6. Day two, morning Mirror Lake and Cat Lake Mirror Lake is 1.5 miles further along #63 at 10,573 feet, with Cat Lake at 10,619 a little north of the corridor. Both are worth an hour with day packs before you strike camp, and doing them light rather than loaded is the whole reason day two is short. Adds about three miles round trip.
  7. Mile 8.1 · 10,391 ft Back at the junction, then west Return up the spur to the Upper Cataract junction and go west on #60. From here the trail descends toward Tipperary Lake — the only sustained downhill in the middle of this loop, and it is 630 feet you climb back.
  8. Mile 9.5 · 9,761 ft Tipperary Lake A tenth of a mile off #60 on a spur, and the low point between the camps. Water, and a reasonable place to stop and eat. Then 520 feet back up to the Eaglesmere junction — the last real climb of the trip.
  9. Mile 10.9 · 10,281 ft The Eaglesmere junction Eaglesmere Trail #61 leaves north here — that is tomorrow's line. Today, continue southwest another 0.6 miles to the lakes. Getting this backwards costs you the camp, not the trip.
  10. Mile 11.5 · 10,391 ft Eaglesmere Lakes — Camp 2 Three tarns in timber at 10,391 feet. Same rules: 100 feet from water and trail, no fires. The Gore Range Trail beyond here runs on toward Elliott Ridge and eventually forty more miles of the range — a different trip.
  11. Miles 12.1–16.5 · down to 9,005 ft Eaglesmere Trail #61 Back to the junction, right and north, and 4.4 miles of descent losing 1,276 feet. The trail leaves the wilderness boundary at about mile 8.35 of the standard loop numbering, and finishes at the Eaglesmere trailhead — which is not where you left the car.
  12. Miles 16.5–17.1 · back to 8,585 ft The road walk Turn left out of the Eaglesmere lot and walk 0.6 miles down Cataract Creek Road, dropping 420 feet, to the Surprise trailhead. It is the one piece of this loop that is not trail, and it is the reason the route is a genuine loop rather than an out-and-back. Sign out if there is a register.

The altitude on night one, and the moose

This route removes the two dangers the other plans were organised around: there is no dry stretch, and there is nowhere above treeline. What is left is smaller but not nothing, and the first item is the one that has a real chance of spoiling the trip.

  • Camp 1 is at 10,752 feet, and you woke up in Austin. That is the highest first night of any plan we have costed — nearly two thousand feet above the Lost Creek camp. Headache, nausea and a sleepless night are the ordinary outcome, not the exotic one. Drink hard on the drive, eat at the trailhead, walk the climb slower than you want to, and if one of you is genuinely unwell, Surprise Lake at 10,057 is a legal camp 2.6 miles back down.
  • Moose, and they are the animal to actually worry about here. The Gore Range holds them and they favour exactly this terrain — willow, lake margins, timbered basins. A moose will not raid your food, and it will not bluff-charge and stop. Give any moose a very wide berth, put a tree between you if one closes, and never get between a cow and a calf. This is a different discipline from bear country, and it is more important on this route than the bear discipline is.
  • No campfires, and not because of the drought. The Eagles Nest order prohibits fires within a quarter mile of any lake, within 100 feet of any stream or trail, and above 11,000 feet — which is every plausible campsite on this loop. On top of that the forest is currently under Stage 2 restrictions with Very High fire danger. Stove only, and confirm the restriction level before you leave.
  • Afternoon storms still happen — you are just able to hide. At 10,400 to 10,750 feet in August, expect them. The difference from every other plan we looked at is that you are in trees when it arrives, and no part of this route commits you to a long exposed crossing. Don't take that as licence to sit on an open lakeshore during a cell.
  • Deadfall and route-finding on the Surprise Trail. A 2024 report through the first 3.3 miles logged significant deadfall and a reroute near mile 2.8. Nothing dangerous, but it slows you down on the day you can least afford it, so budget five hours for six miles rather than four.
  • The spur is the one navigational error available. Miles 6.1 to 8.1 are the same ground walked twice. If you come back to the Upper Cataract junction and turn the wrong way you are heading back to the car, and if you miss the junction entirely on the way out you skip Camp 1. Everything else on this loop is a signed T.

Nothing here requires a dawn start or a water plan. It requires drinking enough on Monday and knowing what to do about a moose.

Before we go

Fires

Stove only, twice over. Eagles Nest prohibits campfires within a quarter mile of any lake, within 100 feet of any stream or trail, and above 11,000 feet — there is no legal fire ring on this route. The White River National Forest is additionally under Stage 2 restrictions with Very High fire danger on the Dillon Ranger District as of 2026-07-29. Re-check the forest alerts page within 48 hours of leaving.

Water

The best of any plan we costed. The route is creekside or lakeside for nearly all of it: Cataract Creek at the start, several crossings on the climb, then Surprise, Upper Cataract, Cat, Mirror, Tipperary and Eaglesmere Lakes. Filter everything anyway. This is the one option where the 2026 drought is not the central problem.

Altitude

Trailhead 8,585 feet, Camp 1 at 10,752, Camp 2 at 10,391, and the high point is Camp 1. There is no acclimatisation day in a three-day trip flown in from sea level, so the mitigation is hydration, a slow first climb, and knowing that Surprise Lake at 10,057 is a legal fallback camp.

The road in

Paved to within 2.4 miles of the trailhead. Cataract Creek Road (FDR 1725) is described as improved dirt suitable for most 2WD cars in good condition — better odds for the cheap rental than Lost Creek's eleven miles of FR-211 or Buffalo Peaks' Weston Pass Road. No 2026 condition report was found.

Permit, fee and rules

Free self-issued wilderness permit at the trailhead, plus $5 for the parking and recreation use, good for five consecutive days — bring cash. Group limit 15. Dogs on leash. Camp 100 feet from water and trail. No bicycles or mechanised anything.

Signal and bugs

No signal past Silverthorne to speak of and none in the wilderness. Someone at home gets the itinerary and the plate. Mosquitoes are reported bad after rain in this drainage — bring repellent.

04 — Mileages come from two different kinds of source and it matters which. The through-legs — 3.3 mi to the Gore Range Trail, 4.1 to the Upper Cataract junction, about 7.0 to the Eaglesmere junction, 12.0 for the standard loop including the road walk — are from a June 2024 trip report that recorded them mile by mile. The spur figures are measured along OpenStreetMap trail geometry: 2.0 mi each way from the junction to Camp 1, and 1.5 mi on to Mirror Lake. I had originally assumed 1.2 mi for that spur; the straight-line distance alone is 1.74 mi, which is impossible for a 1.2-mile trail, and that check is what caught the error. Total 17.1 mi.
05 — OpenStreetMap's Surprise Trail and Eaglesmere Trail are fragmented into disconnected pieces, so I could not measure those two legs along their geometry — 0.87 mi and 3.02 mi respectively stitched, against reported lengths of 3.3 and 4.4. 06 — Wikimedia Commons has no photograph of any lake on this route, and no category for the Eagles Nest Wilderness at all. The two images used here are distant views of the range, captioned as such. Nothing on this page shows the ground you will actually walk.
07 — Fire restrictions for these dates are not knowable today. That is a general figure for the range rather than a surveyed treeline for this specific basin — it holds with about 600 feet to spare, but it is not a measurement.
