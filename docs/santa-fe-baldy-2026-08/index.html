Why here

This is the cheapest, lowest-friction alpine trip available from Austin. One nonstop to Albuquerque, the cheapest class of rental car, and NM-475 is paved the whole way to a trailhead at 10,272 feet — no forest road, no clearance question, no gate to open. Fifteen miles of walking gets a lake at 11,737 feet with Santa Fe Baldy, a 12,631-foot walk-up, directly above camp.

It also suits going alone. The Winsor Trail is the busiest trail in the Sangre de Cristos — a mountain-bike corridor for its first miles and a day-hiker highway to Nambe Lake — which means company, a beaten track, and a short bail-out the entire way. The trade is honest: Lake Katherine is a popular camp and you will not have it to yourself. On a solo trip that is a feature, not a cost.

The diagram is the offline and print fallback — no network, survives a dead phone. The dashed line is straight-line spacing between surveyed waypoints, not the trail. It understates the walking badly: the trailhead sits four straight-line miles from the lake but seven and a half trail miles, because the route drops into the Rio Nambe and climbs back over a pass. Carry the paper sheet.

  1. Mile 0 · 10,272 ft Winsor trailhead, Ski Santa Fe End of NM-475, fifteen miles above town. Upper and lower lots, restrooms, and no wilderness permit needed anywhere in the Pecos. Trail #254 leaves from the left as you pull in. Elevation from the USGS 10-metre DEM at the Forest Service trailhead coordinate; the agency lists the trail topping out at 10,293 ft, which agrees.
  2. Miles 0–1 The only steep half mile A stiff climb to the wilderness boundary fence, then it flattens. At the boundary keep straight — the path breaking right climbs Raven's Ridge, a fine walk and the wrong one today.
  3. Mile 2.4 · the Y Left at the Y The trail has been descending through aspen into the Rio Nambe. At the signed Y, go left for Puerto Nambe and Lake Katherine. Right is the spur to Nambe Lake — the most popular day hike in the range, and a dead end for this trip. Water at the Nambe crossing just past the junction; the footbridge has needed repair in recent years.
  4. Miles 3–4.6 La Vega, then the pass Open meadow with water and, most summers, cattle. Above it the trail climbs steadily to Puerto Nambe. Last easy water before camp is here.
  5. Mile 4.6 · 11,104 ft Puerto Nambe — Camp 1 A broad meadow pass where Skyline Trail #251 splits off. Good dispersed camping in the trees at the edges — 200 feet off water, 50 feet off the trail, per Pecos Wilderness rules. Storms rake this pass in the afternoon; it is not a place to be caught high and late.
  6. Miles 4.6–7.4 Left, and up the switchbacks From the pass, left toward Lake Katherine; right runs to Spirit Lake, which is the sheltered alternative if the weather is ugly and has good established sites of its own. The Katherine line climbs about 630 feet in tight switchbacks under the ridge. The Forest Service numbers the final branch Lake Katherine Trail #242 and gives it 1.3 miles, so roughly half this segment is still on #254 and #251 — trust the signs over the arithmetic.
  7. Mile 7.4 · 11,737 ft Lake Katherine — Camp 2 A cirque lake directly under the north face of Santa Fe Baldy. Camping and campfires are prohibited within 200 feet of the lake, and the Forest Service says plainly that Katherine is heavily impacted and asks people to camp elsewhere where they can — worth taking seriously, and worth arriving early enough to have the choice. Volunteer rangers do check and will move you.
  8. From camp · 12,631 ft Santa Fe Baldy, if the morning is clean Back to the Skyline saddle and up the ridge — a walk-up with no technical difficulty in good conditions, about a thousand feet from the saddle. Wholly exposed. Alone, this is a before-noon proposition with a hard turnaround, no exceptions.
  9. Miles 7.4–15 · back to 10,272 ft The return Reverse it: down the switchbacks, right at Puerto Nambe, past La Vega, across the Nambe, then the one real climb of the day back to the boundary fence and a final easy half mile down to the lot. Four hours or so, and the last stretch has cell signal well before the car.

Exposed ground, alone, in monsoon season

This is an easy trail with one serious hazard: everything above Puerto Nambe is open, high, and directly in the path of the afternoon storms that build here most days in August. Solo removes the margin — nobody to go for help, nobody to argue you out of a bad call.

  • Puerto Nambe and the Baldy ridge by noon. The pass and the summit ridge are both fully exposed. Turn around alone, on schedule, with nobody watching.
  • Satellite messenger on your body, not in the pack, with a check-in someone at home is actually watching. They get the route, the camps, the plate, and a walk-out deadline before you leave.
  • Hang everything scented. Black bears, no grizzlies, no canister rule — a proper hang or Ursack, well away from the tent.
  • Respect the 200-foot rule at the lake. Rangers check Katherine, and a drought year makes the shoreline damage worse. Arrive early enough to have a legal site.
  • Altitude, fast. Austin to 11,100 feet in one afternoon. The short first day is the plan; drink ahead of thirst and treat a headache as information.

The trail's popularity is the safety net here. Use it — camp near other people, and tell someone the plan.

Before we go

Fires

Stage II restrictions (no wood or charcoal fires; stoves only, in a cleared area) were ordered forest-wide June 26 through September 30, 2026 — though the order had dropped off the Santa Fe NF alerts page by July 28 with no rescission announced. Plan stove-only and check the alerts page the week of.

Water

The Rio Nambe crossing, La Vega, and the lake are the reliable sources; the stretch between La Vega and the lake is dry. Filter everything, and top off at La Vega before the pass.

Altitude

The trailhead is already at 10,272 feet — higher than most Colorado trailheads. Camp one at 11,104 is the acclimatization night, and the short first day exists for that reason alone.

The road up

NM-475 is paved, winding, and busy on weekends. The lots at the ski basin fill on August weekends; a Tuesday is fine. No forest road, no clearance question — this is the trip's whole logistical argument.

Signal

There is usually service at the ski basin and on the upper switchbacks, and none in the Nambe drainage or at the lake. Send the itinerary before you start walking.

Camping rules

No permit needed. Camping and campfires prohibited within 200 feet of any lake and within 50 feet of trails and streams. Lake Katherine is heavily used and the Forest Service asks people to spread out — Puerto Nambe and Spirit Lake both hold legal, quieter sites.
